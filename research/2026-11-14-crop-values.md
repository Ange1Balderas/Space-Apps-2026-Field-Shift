## How to read this file

- Five AI research agents each took one part of the question. They were told to report only values they opened and saw in that run, with the exact URL and a table, page or section locator, and never to fill a value from memory.
- **Status** is the researcher's own label: `found` (read at the source), `inference` (derived by the agent from published numbers, not published as such), `not_found`, or `link_broken`.
- **Opened** says whether the researcher reported opening that source and seeing the value during the run.
- A second agent was meant to re-open every source and try to prove each value wrong. **That re-check finished for one part only (USDA dates).** The run was interrupted before the other four parts were re-checked, so treat those values as researched but not independently confirmed.
- Before any value is loaded in Phase 3, the project's `data-verifier` subagent should open its source and confirm it, as CLAUDE.md requires for every change to `data/` or `config/`.
- Values keep the units their sources publish. Nothing has been converted.

## Fallow storage efficiency and effective rainfall fraction

Independent re-check: **not done**.

**Researcher's recommendation for Kansas (an opinion to review, not a source):**

Fallow storage efficiency (western Kansas only): load the K-State Tribune 2008-2019 measurements as defaults because they are the only Kansas-measured, rotation-specific values opened: wheat-fallow 28% (reduced till), wheat-sorghum-fallow 16% before sorghum and 19% before wheat, wheat-corn-fallow 18% before corn and 15% before wheat. Show them with a band rather than as tillage effects, since W-F was reduced till measured gravimetrically and the others no-till by neutron probe. For tillage in wheat-fallow use 17-25% tilled or stubble mulch and about 33-35% no-till, capped at 40% (K-State eUpdate 2013; Akron 1996-2006: 20% conventional, 35% no-till). Where Akron sources conflict, use Nielsen and Vigil (2010), not Smika (1990, 41-49%): Smika's years were wetter than normal and the paper itself says its values overstate the picture, while the 2010 paper reports ten seasons and agrees with K-State's 33% and the 40% ceiling. For no-till wheat-sorghum-fallow use the measured 16-19% (Tribune) and 18% (southwest Kansas), not the 25-30% background statement, which has no dataset behind it; treat 25-30% as an upper bound. No Kansas-measured efficiency by tillage within one rotation was opened, so model conventional tillage as about 1 to 2 inches less stored water at planting (Tribune abstract; Stone and Schlegel 2006). Effective rainfall: do not load a single fraction as a published K-State or USDA value, because none exists in the sources opened. If the tool needs one, compute it monthly with the NRCS handbook method (rain, crop ET, usable soil storage, capped at the lesser of rain and ET) and label it as an irrigation-planning method applied to dryland by extrapolation, likely optimistic on sloping or slowly permeable soils; for daily budgets follow K-State practice (ignore events under 0.2 inch, credit rain only up to the root-zone deficit). Nothing was opened for central or eastern Kansas, so both sets of values should be flagged as unsupported there.
